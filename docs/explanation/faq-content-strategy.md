# FAQ content strategy

Why the FAQ at `/faq/` is organised the way it is. The facts about its files and behaviour are in the [FAQ reference](../reference/faq.md); the sources behind it are in the [FAQ research record](../records/faq-research.md).

## Seven audiences

The FAQ serves the seven audiences of the owner's audit brief of 15 November 2026, each with different starting knowledge. The first rebuild, earlier in November, worked from five needs and eight audiences; the brief merged legacy and current REP holders, folded prediction-market builders into integrators, and added traders, whom the first rebuild had left without a question. Which answers serve which audience, and what is left to other pages, is in the [audit record](../records/faq-audit.md#audience-coverage).

| Audience | What they need first | Where they start |
|---|---|---|
| Original Augur community | Whether it's back, who runs it, what changed since v1 and v2, whether the old markets work | Augur |
| Newcomers | What Augur and an oracle are, why it matters who decides | Augur |
| REP holders | Which token is current, whether migration is still possible (no), what holding earns (nothing passively), forks | $REP |
| Traders | Whether there is anywhere to trade (no), how an outcome is settled, how long it takes, what happens to an ambiguous question | Augur; Augur Lituus |
| Developers and integrators | What exists today (nothing deployed), which questions it can resolve, cost and latency, how an integration would work | Augur Lituus |
| Security researchers and skeptics | Attacks, assumptions, who can intervene, how Augur Lituus compares | Augur Lituus |
| Partners, researchers and observers | Who's behind it, funding, progress, official channels | Augur |

The owner's edits of 15 November 2026 first cut the page to 17 questions, which left traders, legacy REP holders, developers and skeptics without their first question. A review the same day ([record](../records/faq-review.md)) proposed short answers for those needs, and the owner approved them: whether there is anywhere to trade, how an outcome is resolved, what secures the oracle, how an application will integrate it, whether unmigrated REP can still be converted, and whether holders have to act ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#15-november-2026)). Still left to other pages: time to finality, ambiguous questions, the comparison with other oracles, wallet display and how to report a vulnerability.

Each audience has a topic to start in, and each section's name stands beside its questions. A Start here block that routed readers to two questions each was tried and removed on 11 November 2026 ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#11-november-2026)); the section names do that routing instead.

## Topics and order

Three sections, named for the three subjects of the page, since 15 November 2026: Augur, Augur Lituus and $REP ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#15-november-2026)). Before that there were four with one-word names, General, Product, The Token and Builders, as the owner asked on 11 and 13 November. "General" told a reader nothing, "Product" suggested a catalogue, and Builders held one question, which repeated "Is Augur Lituus live?". A section needs at least three questions, so developer questions sit in Augur Lituus until there are that many. Within a section the order runs from what most readers need to what fewer need. The section ids are still `general`, `product` and `rep`, so old links to a section work.

- **Augur**: the project. What Augur is, what an oracle is, whether Augur is back, who is behind it, whether there is anywhere to trade, and its official channels. Newcomers and returning users start here. Questions here are about Augur, the project; anything about Augur Lituus belongs in the next section.
- **Augur Lituus**: what it is, whether it is live, how it resolves an outcome, what secures it, whether anyone can override an outcome, what it can be used for, how an application will integrate it, and how it differs from Augur v1 and v2 and from trading venues such as Polymarket and Kalshi. That last title names Augur Lituus because "Augur" alone could not answer No while the Foundation also funds a prediction market. The longer answers on the mechanism (the four stages, time to finality, Invalid, what a fork is, deterrence, value secured, the guarantee) stay in `parked.ts` for the product page ([FAQ reference](../reference/faq.md#files)).
- **$REP**: its role in Augur, whether holding it earns anything, why there has been more than one token, whether unmigrated REP can still be converted, and where to buy it, which also says where the MiCA white paper stands.

Forks have no question of their own. "How does Augur Lituus resolve an outcome?" names the fork as the last step, where $REP holders migrate to the truthful universe, and the Moon Fork is named in "Is Augur back?" and in the token history, which links the [case study](/learn/fork/moon-fork/). "Do $REP holders need to take any action?", which said what a fork asks of a holder, was removed at the owner's request on 16 November 2026 ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#16-november-2026)).

Old fragments keep working: every earlier topic id opens the section that now holds its questions.

## Choosing the questions

About 115 candidate questions were written from the brief, the old FAQ, the REP page, the blog's announcements, the whitepaper and the competitor research, then merged and cut to 65. On 11 November 2026 the owner asked for about 35, in fewer topics, still covering the questions most readers in each audience ask, and then for REP to have its own topic, for no Security and trust topic and for far less about forks: 35 questions were merged into 30, each keeping its id as an alias ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#11-november-2026)). The rules:

- Every question has a recognisable audience and a reason someone would ask it, from a search, a Discord thread or a misread announcement.
- Two questions that would get the same answer were merged, and the merged id kept as an alias (for example `legacy-rep` into "Which REP token is current?").
- Questions that need a walkthrough, not an answer, stay short and name where the full material lives (the Moon Fork case study, the whitepaper) rather than reproducing it.
- Cut: product names hidden from the live site (Zoltar, Augur Statoblast) and filler such as what the name Lituus means. Dark Florist was cut too, until the owner asked on 15 November 2026 for "Is Augur back?" to name it once, as an independent team the Foundation funds. Where to buy REP was cut at first and added back on 15 November 2026 at the owner's request, naming exchanges as text without links while linking exchanges remains an open decision.

The audit of 15 November 2026 ([record](../records/faq-audit.md)) tested each question against the audiences above: who asks it, how often, and whether another answer already gives the answer. It merged the ticker question into the two that already answered it, replaced two titles that their answers did not match, and added five questions where an audience's main concern had no answer: trading, the difference from Augur v1 and v2, time to finality, ambiguous questions and the Moon Fork. Four of those restore subjects removed on 11 and 13 November, when the aim was a shorter page; the audit's test is coverage, so a question that a trader or an integrator would ask first comes back, shorter than it was. That made 27 questions. The owner then asked which questions a reader would bring that no title answered, and five more were added: whether Augur Lituus is live, who is behind Augur, whether Augur Lituus competes with Polymarket and Kalshi, what Augur Lituus means for REP holders and how much value it can secure. Each was already answered in a sentence somewhere, but a reader scanning titles could not find it. A 33rd, "Why is there more than one REP token?", followed at the owner's request. It presents REP as one token with a line of succession, each step with a named cause, because a list of four contract addresses on its own reads as four tokens. That made 33. The owner then removed two: "Why build a decentralized, permissionless and trustless oracle?" for now, because no draft of its answer satisfied them, and "Can I trade on Augur today?", which left 31, then took seven Product answers off the page for the product page, which left 24, deleted "What was the Moon Fork?", which left 23, removed the comparison with other oracles for now, which left 22, and deleted four Token answers (what Augur Lituus means for holders, which token is current, wallet display and REP held before the Moon Fork), which left 18, and deleted two Builders answers (how an application would integrate Augur Lituus, and how to report bugs and vulnerabilities), which left 16, and added "Does $REP have a MiCA white paper?", which left 17 questions ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#15-november-2026)). A review later the same day ([record](../records/faq-review.md)) found that the cuts had removed the questions with the best evidence of being asked: the live site still answered six of the removed subjects, and about 4.6 million REP had not migrated in the Moon Fork. The owner approved its answers: two merges (the Builders question into "Is Augur Lituus live?" and the MiCA question into the buying answer) and six short additions, which makes 21.

Questions that prevent the most costly misunderstandings are written most carefully: that Augur Lituus isn't live, that holding $REP earns nothing passively and requires no reporting or challenging, that the Moon Fork's migration can't be reopened, and that a buyer should check the ticker and the contract address. The FAQ does not print the address; the buying answer and the token history link the REP page, which shows it.

## How answers are written

The FAQ is written in a professional register, modelled on the DIA and Chainlink FAQs, at the owner's request on 13 November 2026 ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#13-november-2026)): third person, a definition first where the question asks what something is, precise terms (smart contract, onchain, staking) defined once rather than explained through stories, no contractions and no casual asides. Questions are single questions in the same register, such as "What is a blockchain oracle?" and "Is Augur Lituus live?". A question about Augur Lituus names it; "Augur" alone means the project, so a question does not attribute to Augur what only Augur Lituus is designed to do. The references' credibility comes from facts (dates, founders, named mechanisms), so this FAQ keeps its facts and sources and does not borrow their superlatives, which would be unsourced ranking claims. This departs from the site-wide rule to write for traders and beginners ([conventions](../reference/conventions.md#content)) for the FAQ only; beginners are still served by defining each term once.

Each answer starts with the answer, often in one word ("No.", "Not yet."), then gives the detail a reader needs to act, and stops. Each one has to stand on its own: a reader who lands on a single answer from a search gets the whole of it. A link sits on a word in the text only where it's the obvious next step, and never as a button under the answer ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#11-november-2026)). There are eight outside the official channels list, which links every channel it names; most go to the pages that carry what the FAQ leaves out: the whitepaper, the REP page and the Moon Fork case study. Answers average about 71 words, and 17 of the 20 are under 100.

Mechanism is one clause and a link. An answer gives the reason its claim is true in a sentence and links the page that carries the walkthrough, with no stake sizes, rounds, thresholds or windows. This keeps the owner's instruction of 15 November 2026, that the FAQ does not explain how the oracle works step by step, and replaces adjectives such as "trustless" and "permissionless" with what makes them true. The longer mechanism and trust answers, 120–155 words with steps or named assumptions, stay parked for the product page.

Nothing in Augur Lituus is deployed, so an answer that describes how it works says so once, with "under its design", "is designed to" or "will". Each answer is read alone, in search results, in `/faq.md` and in the page's structured data, where "Is Augur Lituus live?" is not beside it. One thing has one name throughout: outcome, stake, response window, fork threshold. Every figure from the whitepaper says so, except the attack cost in "What secures Augur Lituus?" (below), and illustrations are labelled as illustrations. The token is written $REP in every question and answer, at the owner's request of 16 November 2026; the versioned symbols such as REPv1 are token names and keep their spelling. The owner removed the questions on rules, timing, Invalid answers, status, the comparison with Augur v2, Augur Lituus's token and app planning on 11 November 2026 ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#11-november-2026)); timing, Invalid answers and the comparison with Augur v2 came back in the audit of 15 November, status is now "Is Augur Lituus live?", and timing and Invalid answers were parked for the product page later the same day. The code's differences from the paper are still not listed on the page; they remain in the [research record](../records/faq-research.md).

The hard questions get direct answers. No one, the Foundation and the developers included, can change an outcome, and the answer says what does set one and that the Foundation takes part only as a $REP holder. "What secures Augur Lituus?" answers with $REP holders' duty to the truth, the stake a liar forfeits and the cost of corrupting an outcome, given as a plain figure. Until 16 November 2026 it named the whitepaper's authors and the one condition the security rests on; the owner asked for an answer that does not mention the whitepaper or hedge ("estimates", "mentions"), so no answer now carries a caveat, and the figure's source and assumptions are recorded in the entry's `sources`. A question's title does not claim more than its answer. Competitor FAQs that stated controls without limits read as marketing; the ones that named attacks and answered them read as credible.

The FAQ doesn't name, rank or compare other oracles. Its comparison was rewritten on 15 November 2026 around what a lie costs, without naming any oracle, and then removed for now at the owner's request; it is kept in `parked.ts`.

## Why native disclosures, one open at a time

Each question is a native `<details>`, so the page reads, deep-links and finds (Find in page opens a closed answer) without JavaScript, and keyboard and screen-reader behaviour is the browser's own. The cost is that questions aren't headings: assistive technology exposes a `summary` as a button and drops any heading inside it. Topic headings (`h2`) remain for navigation by heading. A custom accordion with headings would need JavaScript for every behaviour and would hide answers from readers without it, which the project's rules exclude.

Questions open one at a time, through the native `name` attribute, at the owner's request ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#11-november-2026)). That keeps the page short while reading. The trade-off is that a reader can't keep two answers open to compare them, and opening a question can move content above it out of view.

## Why there's no search

A search filter, Open all and Copy link were built and then removed on 11 November 2026 ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#11-november-2026)). The browser's Find in page searches closed answers, the three sections are short enough to scroll, and each question keeps its own link, `/faq/#<id>`, for sharing.

## Why the page looks as it does

The layout was settled on 15 November 2026 ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#15-november-2026)). Five alternative layouts were prototyped first: every answer visible, a documentation sidebar, search, topic tabs and a ledger of short answers. The owner rejected all five as noisy and cluttered and asked for a quiet revision of the existing page instead. A version with one centred column was rejected too.

Each section's name sits in the left column beside its questions, on the grid the homepage's sections use, and stays there while the questions scroll. With three sections that does the rail's job, naming where the reader is, so the rail was removed. The cost is that there is no jump from one section to another; collapsed, the three sections are about two screens tall on a desktop.

Questions are filled boxes with space between them, because the owner disliked rules between rows and between sections. The fill steps from the quiet surface to the tile surface on hover and when open, so state is carried by the surface and by the plus turning into a Green minus, and no line or underline is needed. The scroll reveal is the homepage's script, so the two pages move alike and follow the same once-per-session rule.

The page ends with one contact block. The footer's contact band would repeat "Still have a question?" directly beneath it, so this page leaves the band out; the cost is that the footer's newsletter column is not on this page.

## Why the Markdown copy is generated

Other pages' Markdown copies are written by hand ([markdown pages](../reference/markdown-pages.md)). A hand-written copy of 20 answers would drift from the page within one edit, and the FAQ's answers already are Markdown, so `/faq.md` is generated from the same entries. This is the one exception to the hand-written rule.

## Maintenance

Answers about current status carry `timeSensitive`, which readers don't see; they are listed for review with each quarterly report in the [update guide](../guides/update-faq.md), and each answer's `reviewed` date records when it was last checked. An "As of" date on those answers was shown and then removed at the owner's request ([decisions](../records/decisions.md#11-november-2026)). When Augur Lituus deploys, the status, integration and holder answers change most; the proposed-rules answers become reference to the deployed parameters.
